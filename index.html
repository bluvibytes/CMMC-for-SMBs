How this works

Independent and educational. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the Department of Defense (DoD, now also titled the Department of War, or DoW), the Cyber AB, or ISACA. It does not perform or replace a certified assessment, and using it does not affirm CMMC status in SPRS.

It is not meant to replace expertise. It is here to help small and medium businesses cut the overall cost of reaching CMMC. Use it on your own, share it across a team, or run it alongside a paid support service. If you are ever unsure, a CMMC professional can be hired by the hour, like a lawyer, and this tool does not replace that help when your organization needs it.

  1. Set your scope. In the Scope section, say whether you handle FCI or CUI, then map where your data lives. Shrinking that footprint is the biggest money saver.
  2. Work the 59 objectives. For each one, tap Met, Not met, or N/A, add a short note on how, and record your evidence.
  3. Watch the readiness bar. The top of the page tracks progress. Level 1 is pass or fail, so every applicable objective has to be Met.
  4. Export when ready. From Reports, generate your System Security Plan as Word or PDF, or a CSV for tracking.
  5. Save to resume, version, or hand off. Save writes a small, date- and time-stamped file of your answers. Keep each one as a version, or hand it to a teammate or advisor who can Load it and continue, recorded as the updater.

Nothing you type leaves your device. This page is the tool; the saved file is just your answers, so you can stop and continue later or hand it to a teammate.

i

Start here. Level 1 is the 15 safeguards in FAR 52.204-21 (renumbered FAR 52.240-93 in the 2026 FAR overhaul; the same 15 controls), and the rule grades them as 59 yes-or-no objectives. A practice counts as met only when every one of its objectives is met. You assess yourself once a year and affirm in SPRS. No outside auditor is required at this level.

$

Where small businesses overpay. Level 1 does not assess IoT, operational technology, government-furnished equipment, test equipment, or restricted information systems. They are out of scope. The other big lever is your footprint: every system FCI touches has to meet all 17 practices, so shrinking what touches FCI shrinks the whole job. Map it in the Scope section before you get a quote.

!

If you handle CUI, Level 1 is not your whole assessment. CUI pulls you up to Level 2 and usually a third-party C3PAO. Level 1 is still the foundation Level 2 builds on (the 17 practices here are a subset of the 110 at Level 2), so this is a useful starting point, not a substitute. This is not anti-consultant either: use it before or alongside paid help so you are not over-scoped.

First, are you even Level 1?

What is FCI? What is CUI?

FCI, Federal Contract Information. Information provided by or generated for the government under a contract that is not meant for public release, such as nonpublic contract details, schedules, or deliverables. If FCI is the most sensitive thing you hold, you are Level 1, and this is the right tool.

CUI, Controlled Unclassified Information. Information that a law, regulation, or government-wide policy requires you to safeguard. Holding CUI puts you at Level 2, not Level 1. Common examples for a contractor: CAD, engineering, or technical data (CTI); export-controlled data (ITAR or EAR); proposal, bid, or source-selection information; or anything your contract marks CUI, so look for DFARS 252.204-7012.

The government decides what is CUI, through your contract and the markings on what you receive. The full list is the federal CUI Registry. If any apply, this tool is useful groundwork, but it is not your Level 2 assessment.

A tool cannot make the judgment calls for you. If you are unsure whether something truly counts as Met, whether your evidence is enough, or even whether you hold FCI versus CUI, that is a question for a person, not a checkbox. This tool does not warrant or guarantee CMMC approval, and it does not replace professional services your organization may need. It exists to help small and medium businesses make informed decisions, with plain-English explanations of the terms and requirements, and to reduce costs where you can do the work yourself. Three cheap habits that map to Revision 3

These cost little to start now and line up with where NIST SP 800-171 is heading in Revision 3, so starting early means less to retrofit later. Track each one below; if the report box above is checked, your status and notes ride along as an appendix.

Why Revision 2, not Revision 3

CMMC is still assessed against NIST SP 800-171 Revision 2 today. Revision 3 is the newer version, and it is not yet required for CMMC. When you open the Revision 2 page at NIST, you will see a banner saying it was withdrawn and superseded by Revision 3. That is NIST's catalog housekeeping, not a CMMC change: the CMMC rule (32 CFR Part 170) still points to Revision 2, so it remains the correct baseline for your assessment.
